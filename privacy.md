# Privacy Policy — Outfitted

**Last updated: 20 September 2026**

Outfitted is a wardrobe app. You photograph clothes you own, it organises them,
and an assistant called Max suggests outfits and shows you wearing them. This
explains what that involves for your data.

**Who is responsible (data controller):** Chris Leszczynski, Poland.
**How to reach us:** krzysiu.leszcz@outlook.com

This policy works together with the [Terms of Use](terms.md).

---

## What we collect

**Your account.** An email address and a username. You can change the username
at any time; it is public.

**Your clothes.** Photographs of garments you add, plus what you or Max record
about them: category, colours, brand, pattern, price, season, any tags you
write yourself, and any notes.

**Things you want.** Your wishlist: what you called each thing, the address of
the page you pasted, the address of that page's own picture, the shop's name
and price where the page gives them, and whether you have bought it. The
picture itself is never copied to us — it loads from the shop.

**Pieces that have left.** When you mark a piece as sold, given away, worn out
or lost, the piece and its photo are deleted and a line is kept: its type,
brand, price, when you added it, when it went and why. It is what lets the app
tell you how long your clothes last.

**Photos of you.** A selfie or full-body photo, when you use styling or try-on,
and your profile photo if you set one.

**How you use your wardrobe.** Outfits you save, days you plan them for, and the
cost-per-wear figures worked out from those.

**Looks friends make for you.** If you share your closet, a look a friend puts
together is stored as the list of your own pieces they chose and anything they
wrote with it. Both of you can see it; nobody else can.

**Whether a closet photo has been checked.** Only once you share your closet:
each photo is put through the same check a photo must pass to be posted, and
the result — passed or held back — is stored against the piece.

**What you write.** Your bio, your "currently" answers, post descriptions,
comments, and your conversations with Max, which are kept so a conversation can
continue.

**What you post.** Photos or videos you publish to the feed are **public** —
visible to anyone using the app, not only to people who follow you.

**Social activity and music.** Who you follow, likes, reposts, posts you save,
votes in polls, blocks and reports; songs you like, playlists and recently
played songs.

**Plan and usage.** Your plan, how many metered features (cut-outs,
upscales, try-ons) you have used this month, and how many times today you have
used Max's AI features (chat, identifying clothes, outfit ideas and post
checks), which is how the daily limits are kept. When subscriptions are available,
payment is handled entirely by Apple — we receive whether your subscription is
active, never your card details.

**Technical data.** Our hosting providers keep standard request logs, including
IP addresses, for security and troubleshooting.

**Your approximate location — only if you turn on weather.** To show today's
weather and pick clothes for it, your phone rounds its position to about a
kilometre and sends that straight to MET Norway for the forecast. We never
receive your location, store it, or link it to your account. What Max is told
when he picks an outfit is the weather itself — temperature, rain, wind — not
where you are.

For a trip on your calendar, the same applies to where it is going: the
destination you typed is looked up by your phone's own map service (Apple's on
an iPhone, Google's on Android) to find the place, and that place's rounded
position goes to MET Norway for its forecast. We never receive either.

We do **not** collect your precise location, contacts or advertising
identifiers, we do not track you across other apps or websites, and the app
contains no advertising or analytics SDKs.

## Why we use it (legal bases)

- **To provide the app you signed up for** (contract): storing your closet,
  running Max, showing your posts, planning outfits, managing a subscription.
- **To keep Outfitted safe** (legitimate interests): checking photos before they
  are published, acting on reports and blocks, preventing abuse and securing the
  service.
- **Where the law requires it** (legal obligation): for example, responding to
  lawful requests.
- **Your consent**: your approximate location, for the weather. You give it by
  allowing location when you turn weather on, and you can withdraw it at any
  time in your phone's settings.

We do **not** sell your data, and we do not use it to train AI models.

## Where it goes

| What | Who processes it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Everything you store in the app | **Supabase** — database, sign-in and file storage, hosted in the EU | To run the service |
| Garment photos, photos of you for try-on, messages to Max with a summary of your wardrobe, photos you try to publish | **Max, on servers we operate** | To recognise clothes, answer you, create try-on images and check photos against the rules |
| The same kinds of data, **only when our own Max server is unavailable** | **Anthropic** (Claude API) | The same purposes |
| Song searches, the song player, and the words you type into shop search | **Google** (YouTube Data API, the embedded player, and the Custom Search API behind shop results) | To find and play music on posts, and to find clothes to buy |

| A page address you paste into the board or the wishlist — fetched by your phone, not by us | **That website** | To read the picture the page offers for sharing |
| Your approximate location (rounded to about 1 km), only when weather is on — sent directly from your phone | **MET Norway**, the Norwegian Meteorological Institute, in the EEA | To get the forecast |
| The name of a trip's destination, only when weather is on — looked up on your phone | **Apple** or **Google**, through your phone's built-in map service | To find the place, for its forecast |

Anthropic and Google may process data outside the EU, under safeguards such as
the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses. Their own policies:
[Supabase](https://supabase.com/privacy) ·
[Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy) ·
[Google](https://policies.google.com/privacy) ·
[YouTube Terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms) ·
[Apple](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/) ·
[MET Norway](https://api.met.no/doc/TermsOfService).

The rough carbon figures in Insights are averages published by ADEME, the
French environment agency, for one garment of each kind. They are not a
measurement of your own clothes, and nothing about your closet is sent to
ADEME — the figures are built into the app.

Weather data comes from MET Norway under the
[CC BY 4.0 licence](https://api.met.no/doc/License); the app summarises it
(current conditions and today's range) rather than showing it unchanged.

Songs play through YouTube's official player, on its privacy-enhanced host
(youtube-nocookie.com), which holds off on cookies and profile-building until
somebody presses play. We store only a video's identifier, title, channel and
thumbnail — never the audio or video itself.

When you use shop search, the words you type go to Google's Custom Search API,
through our own Max server, to find matching items, and they appear in the
links to the shops. We do not keep what you searched for. Opening a result
takes you to that shop, under its own privacy policy.

## Who can see what

**Public:** your username, profile photo, bio, "currently" answers, follower and
following counts, your posts (photo or video, caption, song), comments you
write on posts, which posts you repost — a repost shows the post, still
credited to its author, to the people who follow you — and the pieces you tag
on a post: each one's photo, type, colours, brand and pattern.

**Only if you turn closet sharing on:** your closet stays private unless you
choose "Friends" in Closet sharing, and friends means people you follow who
follow you back. Then those people, and only those people, can see the pieces
whose photos passed the check — the photo, the type, the colours, the brand
and the pattern. What a piece cost, the tags you gave it and your notes are
never sent to them at all. Any single piece can be kept back on its own page,
blocking someone closes it immediately, and so does either of you unfollowing.
Turning it back to "Only me" closes it for everyone.

**Private to you:** your closet, your wishlist, saved outfits, saved posts, how you voted in
polls (others see only the totals, the poll's author included), calendar plans,
conversations with Max, saved songs and playlists, and who you have blocked or
reported. Other users cannot list, read or change these; this is enforced by the
database, not only by the app.

**Your photos stay private.** Your closet photos, photos you send to Max,
photos used for styling or try-on, and the images Max makes from them are
stored privately. The app shows them to you through links signed for your
account that stop working after an hour; other users, and anyone who comes
across an old link, cannot open them. A photo becomes public only when you
publish it as a post, which puts a copy in the public feed, or tag the piece it
shows on a post, which makes a public copy of that one piece's photo. Nothing
else of your closet is shown, and a piece's notes and price never are.

**Pictures you find on the web** — pasted into the outfit board or the wishlist
— are read by your phone, which asks that website directly for the page and
takes the picture address the page offers for sharing. The website sees the
request as it would any visit, including your IP address; we never receive the
page or the picture, and nothing is copied into our storage. A picture found
this way sits on your board to work from and is left out of anything you post,
because it belongs to whoever took it.

**Selling a piece** opens Vinted, Vestiaire Collective or Depop in your
browser, under that site's own privacy policy. Outfitted lists nothing for you,
sends them nothing about you, and takes no part in the sale.

**Sharing a post** hands a link to its photo or video, and the author's
username, to the app you pick from your phone's share sheet. What that app
does with it is under its own privacy policy. The link points at the post, so
it stops working if the author deletes it.

The sample-closet photos are provided by us.

## What the app asks permission for

**Camera** — to photograph a garment, take a photo of you for try-on, or record
a video post.
**Photo library** — to pick photos you already have, and to save a result back.
**Microphone** — only when recording a video post with sound.
**Notifications** — optional, to show the outfit you planned for today and to
remind you about occasions on your calendar. Reminders are scheduled on your
phone itself: nothing is sent to us, or anyone else, to deliver them, and
turning them off in your profile removes them from the phone.
**Location** — optional, approximate, and only while you are using the app: to
show today's weather and pick clothes for it. The app asks only when you turn
weather on.

The app never uses your location in the background, and does not request your
contacts or calendar.

## How long we keep it

Your data stays until you delete it. Deleting an item removes it and its photo;
deleting a post removes its photo or video. Saying a piece is gone deletes the
piece and its photo and keeps the line described above; deleting your account
removes that too.

**Delete Account**, in your profile, permanently removes your account, clothes,
photos, outfits, plans, posts, comments, likes, reposts, saved posts, poll
votes, follows, your wishlist, the record of pieces that have left, looks
friends made for you, and conversations with Max. It cannot be undone. Copies in our
providers' backups, where they exist, are overwritten after a short period.

Sample-closet accounts are temporary and may be removed at any time. We may keep
a record of reports and the action taken on them for as long as needed to keep
the community safe and to meet legal obligations.

## Your rights

If you are in the EU or UK, you can ask for a copy of your data, and to correct,
delete, restrict or port it, or object to how it is used. Much of this you can
do in the app; for the rest, email **krzysiu.leszcz@outlook.com** and we will
answer within one month. You can also complain to your national data protection
authority.

## Children

Outfitted is not for children under **13**. If you are under **16** and live in
the EU, you need permission from a parent or guardian. If you believe a child is
using Outfitted without that, contact us and we will remove the account.

## Security

Traffic is encrypted in transit, and access to private data is enforced at the
database level. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise otherwise.

## Changes

If this policy changes, we will update the date above and tell you in the app if
the change is significant.
